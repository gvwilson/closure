Succession Planning

The Scenario

  • You are leaving a project that you currently lead
  • It will carry on after your departure
  • You have time to make a succession plan

How Leaders Leave

  • Four departure archetypes
  • Which one applies is shaped by how central the project is to your identity
  • Recognizing the pattern shapes the handover conversations
  • Monarchs refuse to leave until forced out and undermine successors
  • Generals depart reluctantly and often attempt to return
  • Ambassadors leave willingly and remain available
    • The most satisfied after leaving
  • Governors leave cleanly for new pursuits and disengage entirely

Before You Go

  • Be sure you mean it
    • Letting go will be hard on you
    • Not letting go will be harder on your successors
  • Do it when others think it is time
    • You are often the last to realize you should move on
    • Ask people and pay attention
    • Know people who will tell you the truth
  • Explain what, when, and why
    • Tell people as soon as you are sure
    • Prevents rumors and speculation
  • Do not pick a successor by yourself
    • You are not objective
    • The project’s needs have probably changed
    • Involving others builds community

During the Handover

  • Write a succession plan
    • Final state: what is your successor responsible for?
    • Transition: what are you handing over, and when?
    • First draft alone, final draft with your successor
  • Hand over gradually if you can
    • Share tasks before you leave
    • Go on holiday for a week and leave them in charge
  • Hand over permissions step by step
    • Transfer access a step ahead of the responsibilities
    • Keep at least two people with each critical credential
  • Have some fun before you go
    • People remember beginnings and endings
    • Do one final small project

After You Leave

  • When you leave, leave
    • A continued role confuses people who look to you for answers
  • Leave for something
    • Have your next project lined up, even if small
  • Reflect on what you learned
    • Helps you move on
    • Helps with the next thing
    • Helps your successor and others
  • Celebrate the good things
    • Write down what you accomplished
    • As important as the could-have-beens
