Introduction

Endings Are Real

  • For every beginning there must be an ending
  • But we do not like to talk about endings
    • Thousands of books on starting a business
    • Only a handful on passing one on or winding one down
    • Many of those are really about selling out at the right time
    • Because mortality scares us
  • Preparing for endings can make them less painful
    • And less likely
    • Thinking about how things might end can prevent some endings
  • It also makes what happens before more enjoyable
    • Satisfied business owners started planning years in advance

Learner Personas

  • Jun, 38, a data scientist who released an open source project
    • A reorg moved her to another division
    • She wants to hand the project to a junior team member
  • Vaida, 33, a data analyst and co-founder of a volunteer teaching group
    • Relocating for a new job
    • Wants to wind things down properly
  • Chetan, 27, maintains an online hummingbird tracking site
    • The hosting service was hacked
    • Wants to resurrect it before fall migration
  • Liam, 29, a software developer whose tool is being halted
    • Has until the end of the week to wind things down

The Grid

  • Organize thoughts on a 2x2 grid
  • One axis is continuity
    • Does the project continue after the event?
  • The other axis is pace
    • Is the event gradual or abrupt?
  • Four quadrants, each matched to a persona
    • Succession planning: continues under new leadership (Jun)
    • Deliberate closure: does not continue but there is time (Vaida)
    • Disaster recovery: abrupt interruption but recovers (Chetan)
    • Abrupt closure: shuts down on short notice (Liam)
  • First, foundations that all scenarios rely on
  • Finally, a section on looking after your people

A Word on Advice

  • An abstinence solution requires people to do things they actually will not do
  • “Document everything” is an example
    • People short-change writing in favor of doing
    • Most of what we can write today does not address tomorrow’s needs

Threat Models

  • Your situation usually depends on external factors
    • Use threat modeling to think about them
  • Individual threats affect one or a few team members
    • Career or life changes
    • External factors such as visas revoked with little warning
  • Leadership threats target the project leader
    • Example: media targeting for unpopular research
  • Community threats affect large groups
    • Example: a department being shut down
    • Too many people are hit to buffer the shock
    • Climategate damaged the whole climate research community
  • Global threats affect everyone at the same time
    • Other groups cannot help
    • There may be nowhere to run

Not Covered

    • Assumes you must use the community you already have
  • Communication skills
    • Focus on what to say rather than how to say it
  • For-profit businesses
    • Assumes the project is not meant to turn a profit
