Caring For Your People

Why Care Matters

  • Projects end, but relationships between people do not have to
  • People who depended on you are hit by multiple shocks at once
    • Just like you
  • Acknowledge that shutdown is a loss, even when voluntary
    • People who spent years on a project deserve recognition

Total Pain

  • Total pain describes how a dying patient’s suffering is never purely physical
    • Physical, psychological, social, and spiritual dimensions
    • They interact and amplify one another
  • Project closure is more than logistics
    • Identity loss, grief over unfinished work, anxiety, guilt or anger
    • These can fracture long-standing relationships
  • Treating closure as purely administrative leaves people worse off

Psychological First Aid

  • Five principles guide early crisis response
    • Promote a sense of safety
    • Calm the emotional environment
    • Build self-efficacy and collective efficacy
    • Foster connectedness
    • Maintain hope
  • First conversations after a closure should not be about logistics
    • Confirm people are not in immediate danger
    • Acknowledge what happened rather than minimizing it
    • Remind people they have handled difficult things before
    • Connect people with one another
    • Identify what is uncertain versus decided
  • Sometimes you have to be honest enough

Two Kinds of Coping

  • Problem-focused coping addresses the situation itself
    • Used when the stressor feels controllable
  • Emotion-focused coping addresses the internal response
    • Used when it feels uncontrollable
  • Abrupt closure combines both
    • Team members respond differently depending on their sense of agency
  • The person triaging data is not more competent than the one absorbing the news
    • They are applying different coping modes
  • Managing closure well means making space for both

Grief and Its Timing

  • Anticipatory mourning is grief that begins before an expected loss
    • Processing past, present, and future losses at once
    • More demanding than grief after the fact
    • People must keep functioning while losing the thing they are losing
  • Planned shutdown triggers this in teams
    • Naming the dynamic helps people manage it
  • Grief after a failed venture scales with personal identification
    • And interferes with learning from the experience
  • Grief recovery is not linear
    • Do not force lessons-learned too early
    • A retrospective a month later captures more than one the week it closes

Practical Care

  • Give downstream users notice proportional to their dependence
  • Help the community find each other outside your infrastructure
    • A mailing list or forum they control survives your shutdown
    • Mutual aid builds capacity rather than dependence
  • Teams that kept social contact were more likely to reform
    • Those that dispersed without ties were not
  • Do not trap engineers maintaining a cancelled project
  • People may feel liberated rather than bereft
    • Give permission to experience this without guilt
  • Writing about difficult experiences helps
  • Small acts of recognition matter disproportionately
    • Naming achievements, thanking contributors publicly
  • A goodbye ceremony, however small, marks the ending
    • Closing a project is not erasing it
  • Continuing bonds are a normal, healthy form of grief
    • Framing closure around what continues reduces distress
    • Archiving and attribution become preservation, not compliance
