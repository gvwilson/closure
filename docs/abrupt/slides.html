Abrupt Closure

The Scenario

  • Worst case
    • The project does not continue
    • You get one shot to close it down

What People Actually Do

  • Under pressure, experts do not compare options
    • They recognize a familiar situation and act on the first workable course
    • Recognition-primed decision making
  • Experience from other domains can be misleading
    • But also more useful than people realize
    • Ask “has anyone seen something like this before?”
  • Attention narrows under acute stress
    • Harder to hold multiple competing priorities in mind
    • Teams focus on the most visible problem and forget whole categories
    • A predictable feature of how the brain responds to pressure

Convergence as a Resource

  • Four types of groups form in emergencies
    • Established organizations that stick to their routines
    • Organizations that extend their routines to the new situation
    • Ad hoc groups modeled on established organizations
    • Emergent groups with no prior or subsequent existence
  • People converge on a crisis without being told
    • They step forward because the project mattered
    • They help if given minimum viable information
    • In software, this means unofficial mirrors and community threads
  • Support this if you can without endangering yourself

A Loose Interpretation of the Rules

  • Frontline workers routinely depart from formal rules
    • When applying them mechanically would produce worse outcomes
    • Street-level bureaucracy: real policy is made in the moment
  • Waiting for approval that cannot be obtained is itself a decision
    • Usually a worse one
  • When procedures do not apply, the people present should act within their discretion
    • Copying data, setting up a forwarding address, sending a brief notice
  • Members of marginalized groups often have more practice with hostile bureaucracies
    • Their tactics can be instructive

Pause First

  • Surgical failures were usually failures to perform known steps, not failures of skill
    • A checklist, not more training, was the fix
    • The same principle applies to abrupt closure
  • First, establish how much time you have and what constraints are non-negotiable
    • Keeping shutdown secret backfires
    • Rumors and information vacuums are more destructive than bad news
  • Second, write down every category of thing to address before resolving any
    • Access, backups, notifications, communication, legal obligations
    • The list does not need to be complete or ordered
    • It just needs to exist to prevent tunnel vision
  • Third, triage ruthlessly with MoSCoW
    • Must do: obligations with legal or financial risk
    • Should do: the minimum viable record of the work
    • Could do: a way for the community to find each other
    • Won’t do: everything else, including “do it properly”

Then Speak

  • Three communication errors increase reputational damage
    • Silence: stakeholders fill the vacuum with rumor
    • Denial: asserting the crisis is not serious
    • Diminishment: acknowledging it but minimizing severity
  • Rapid, honest, minimal communication is best
    • Even when you do not yet know what you will do
    • One paragraph: what we know, what we do not know, when we expect more
    • Not a plan, but it prevents the harms of silence

Then Act

  • You probably cannot clean up code, data, or publications
    • A README and a runnable script are a viable minimum
  • Notify funders and stakeholders with binding claims before going dark
  • Provide a forwarding address
    • A working email beats silence
  • Do not promise maintenance you cannot deliver
  • The first conversations are not about logistics
    • The next section covers the human side

When You Are the One Being Hit

  • Most participants are not the ones making the decision
  • If your own work is about to disappear, do the minimum viable handover
    • Push a copy somewhere public
    • Export issues and notes
    • Hand credentials to a trusted person if it is safe
  • Pause before speaking or posting publicly
    • Have someone you trust review it first
